**Changed:**
- The Choose… button in New Session opens at the folder in the Folder field, else the last folder you chose with it or started a session in, else your home folder. Before, it opened at Downloads.

**Fixed:**
- A session you /clear stays in the app. Focus stays on it, now under its new id. Before, Focus could close on "This session is no longer listed" a moment after the /clear, and a /clear the session reported a moment late was missed and the session lost.
- A session you /clear more than once keeps updating in the app: its turn, Needs you and cards follow it after every /clear. Before, from the second /clear on, the app stopped taking the session's reports and its card froze while it went on working.
- Clicking a notification opens its session, including a while after it was shown, and from Notification Center after the app restarts. The click opens Focus only for a session the app last saw running; for any other, the app says it isn't running and offers Resume. Before, a click often only brought the app forward.
- Accounts with no 5-hour limit: the bar now shows 7d and the weekly model limit as 1.1.1 meant to, even when older usage files on the Mac still hold a 5-hour window from before. When Claude Code's /usage reports no 5-hour window, a 5-hour reading whose reset has passed no longer counts. In 1.1.1 such a reading kept the bar on 5h, shown as a dash.
- The usage reading every few minutes no longer waits 3 seconds for input it never gets.
- Resuming a session from the app runs, even within 15 seconds of an earlier resume, when the session stopped or was refused in between. Before, the app answered that it had resumed it and left it stopped.

**Known limits:**
- If a session is stopped outside the app and resumed from the app before the app's next look at the session list, a few seconds later, the resume is still taken as a repeat of the earlier one and doesn't run. Resume it again a moment later.
- The app checks a session's saved settings, which Claude Code keeps in the session's `state.json`, when it sees a new process for the session. A program running as you could change those settings, restart the session from the command line, and put the settings back before the app's next check a few seconds later. The app doesn't catch that: the process runs on the changed settings, and the app finds the original ones. A later release will watch the saved settings for changes.
